WHY KAZAKHSTAN — TWO REASONS THIS DESTINATION WON
-------------------------------------------------

We looked hard at the Alps and at Japan. Kazakhstan beat both on the two
things that actually decide whether a group of thirty people makes it onto a
mountain together.

REASON ONE: VISA-FREE FOR INDIAN PASSPORTS

No application. No embassy appointment. No biometrics, no fee, no three-week
wait with your passport sitting in someone else's drawer. Indian passport
holders enter Kazakhstan visa-free for stays of up to 14 days — comfortably
more than the five days this trip needs. You book a flight and you go.

If you have ever tried to get thirty people Schengen appointments in January,
you already understand why this mattered more than anything else on the list.

REASON TWO: SOME OF THE BEST SKIING OUTSIDE THE ALPS AND THE ROCKIES

Shymbulak is not a novelty. It is a full-scale, modern resort that hosted the
Asian Winter Games, and it sits 25 minutes from a city of two million people:

  * Base at 2,260 m, lifts to 3,200 m at Talgar Pass — roughly a kilometre of
    vertical, on dry continental snow
  * Modern gondolas straight up from Medeu, English-speaking instruction, full
    rental fleet — everything a first-timer needs and enough mountain for
    anyone who isn't
  * A day pass runs about Rs 2,250 and gear hire about Rs 1,050 a day — a
    fraction of Alpine or Colorado pricing
  * No punishing transfer at the far end: sleep in the city, ski the mountain,
    be back for dinner

And the part nobody expects: Almaty is roughly a three-hour flight from Delhi.
Closer than most of Europe, cheaper than all of it, and a genuinely good city
— Soviet bones, Silk Road appetite, mountains at the end of every street.
