What is the IIMA Snow Trek?

A once-a-year excuse to get the batch back together — on snow, off campus, and off the group chat.

🏔️

Edition I: Cervinia

Last year's inaugural Snow Trek took a crew of IIMA alumni to Breuil-Cervinia, under the Matterhorn on the Italian side of the Alps — the trip that started the tradition and set the bar for what "the Snowflake" should feel like.

🇰🇿

Edition II: Kazakhstan

This year we're headed to Central Asia: based out of Almaty with day trips up to Shymbulak, one of the region's best-connected ski resorts — just 25 minutes from the city.

🎿

Built for learning to ski

Group lessons for first-timers, real mileage for anyone with experience, and plenty of non-snow experiences and evenings to unwind — this is a proper winter adventure, not just a ski holiday.

The other half

What you'll find when you come down the mountain

Almaty is a genuinely underrated city — Soviet bones, Silk Road appetite, mountains at the end of every street. The skiing is the reason we're going; this is the reason people come back.

🍲

Eat like you mean it

Beshbarmak, laghman, plov and samsa, and the horse-meat-and-fermented-milk end of the menu if you're game. Central Asian food is its own thing — not quite Russian, not quite Chinese, not quite Turkish, and better than all the guidebooks suggest.

🧺

The Green Bazaar

Almaty's food heart since 1875, and the best afternoon on the trip that doesn't involve snow. Dried fruit, spice mountains, horse sausage, and traders who will absolutely make you taste things. Anything you buy here goes straight into a local trader's pocket rather than a mall's.

🧖

Banya, properly

The traditional bathhouse is a Central Asian institution and the correct answer to legs that have skied for two days. Steam, cold plunge, birch branches, repeat. Not for everyone; unforgettable for the people it's for.

Try Fall Line first

A browser ski game built for this trip: an endless alpine descent where you slow down by turning across the hill, the way you actually do on snow. It's not a lesson — there's a real one waiting for you at Shymbulak — but it's a good ten minutes, and it needs a keyboard, so play it on a laptop.
